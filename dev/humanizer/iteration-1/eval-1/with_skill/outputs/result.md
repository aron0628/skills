# Retention turns on the first 30 days

We built a stronger onboarding process and early-stage engagement improved. Mid-lifecycle churn has not moved. The question is where the next dollar goes, and my answer is the first 30 days of a customer's life, funded as one program rather than five separate fixes.

## Our diagnosis stops at a threshold we have not defined

Our data points one way. Customers who do not reach what we call the "activation threshold" inside their first 30 days are more likely to churn. That is the most useful thing we currently know about retention, and it is also where our knowledge stops: the threshold is not defined anywhere. Until it is, we cannot say how many customers miss it, how much revenue sits behind them, or whether closing the gap is worth the engineering time. Retention measures product-market fit, which is why I am not bringing this as a customer success problem.

Five things may be contributing, and only one of them has a measured trend behind it.

Time to value is the largest group. Users face too many configuration options at setup, time-to-first-value runs longer than competitors', and key capabilities sit behind navigation that does not lead anyone to them. Three of the five candidates describe the same gap between signing up and getting something back.

Support load is second, and it is the item with a measurement attached. Ticket resolution times have been trending upward. We have the direction. We do not have the size.

Mid-tier pricing perception is third. Some segments see limited incremental value at that tier, and we have nothing behind that but their word.

## Three complementary workstreams, funded as one program

- Shorten the activation path. Behavioral analytics can show us where users disengage, so we intervene at those moments instead of at renewal.
- Make retention everyone's target. Retention is everyone's responsibility, not only the customer success team's remit.
- Flag at-risk accounts early. Machine learning may let us catch accounts before churn becomes inevitable.

## What I am asking for

Two things. Agree that we fund this as one program rather than five line items, which is the only decision I need from this memo. And let me come back with the activation threshold defined and the cohort sized behind it, because until that exists any number we attach to this program is a guess. I will circulate next steps and timelines separately.
